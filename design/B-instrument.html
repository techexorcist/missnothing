Direction B — Instrument Dark-first · status-first · typographic · alarms as objects

An alternative to Direction A, not a recolour. Same fourteen surfaces, same content from gold-01 and gold-02 — but a different metaphor, a different default mode, and one structural change to the information architecture.

Thesis. This is not a mail app with alarms bolted on; it is an instrument. An alarm clock knows one thing and states it without being asked. So the home screen is not an inbox of proposals to triage — it is a full-bleed answer to "what does my kid need tomorrow", with the armed alarms visible beneath it, exactly as a clock app shows you which alarms are set. Review moves to a secondary surface, because triage is admin and admin is not the product.

Dark by default, because the two moments that matter are 20:00 and 06:15 and both happen in a dark room. Near-monochrome, with one signal colour that only ever means "you must act" — so it cannot become decoration.

What carries over unchanged

These are product rules, not style, so both directions obey them:

  • The item is the headline — never a festival name, never a circular number.
  • Nothing hides: couldn't-read and sync-incomplete are destinations with counts.
  • Sanitise at render only; stored text stays the school's exact words.
  • Type is never signalled by colour alone — here it's a mono word plus a border.

Three ideas from B worth stealing even if you pick A

  • Alarms as visible, toggleable objects. "Are my alarms actually set?" is the question that makes or breaks trust in this app, and neither a card queue nor a calendar answers it. This is the strongest single idea in either direction.
  • Statement-first home. Opening on the answer rather than a to-triage queue means the app is useful even on a day you have no intention of tidying anything.
  • Edit screen shows rejected dates. "15 August — rejected, letterhead" turns Edit into a window on the parser and will save you debugging time all term.

Honest risk

B is more likely to impress you and less likely to charm a co-parent. If the second installer is your wife rather than another engineer, A's warmth is doing real work and B's precision is mostly doing work for you. The hybrid worth considering is A's palette and voice on B's architecture — statement home, visible alarms, list triage.
