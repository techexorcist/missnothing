Three new skins here — Aurora, Notebook, Dawn/Dusk — plus the three earlier directions, all driving the same three screens. Nothing in the markup knows which skin is active.

That is the answer to your second question. A skin toggle is genuinely cheap — but only for tokens: colour, type, radius, shadow, borders, background. It cannot swap architecture. Direction B's statement-first home and Direction A's card queue are different widget trees, not different themes.

So the shippable version is: one layout, many skins. Pick the architecture once, then let the toggle carry as many looks as you like.

Can the app ship several looks with a Settings toggle?

Yes, and it's one of the cheapest features you could add — provided you keep the line between a skin and a layout.

A skin can changeA skin cannot change
Examples Colour, gradients, type family and weight, corner radius, border width, shadow, light/dark, iconography weight, lock-screen background Which screen is home, whether triage is a stack or a list, whether alarms are visible, tab structure, navigation, what a gesture does
Cost Effectively zero once the token contract exists. A new skin is one file of values. A second widget tree, a second set of tests, and twice the surface for bugs.
Verdict Ship as many as you enjoy. Pick one. Revisit in v2 if you must.

How it works in Flutter

Material 3 already has the mechanism — ThemeExtension. Define the token contract once, then a skin is a value object:

@immutable
class MnTokens extends ThemeExtension<MnTokens> {
  const MnTokens({
    required this.dated, required this.undated, required this.decision,
    required this.urgent, required this.armed,
    required this.cardRadius, required this.borderWidth,
    required this.cardShadow, required this.lockGradient,
    required this.displayFamily,
  });
  final Color dated, undated, decision, urgent, armed;
  final double cardRadius, borderWidth;
  final List<BoxShadow> cardShadow;
  final Gradient lockGradient;
  final String? displayFamily;
  // copyWith + lerp omitted — lerp is what animates the change.
}

enum AppSkin { hearth, instrument, riso, aurora, notebook, dawnDusk }

final skinProvider = StateNotifierProvider<SkinController, AppSkin>(...);

ThemeData themeFor(AppSkin skin, Brightness b) => ThemeData(
  colorScheme: ColorScheme.fromSeed(seedColor: _seed[skin]!, brightness: b),
  extensions: [_tokens[skin]!],
);

Then every widget reads Theme.of(context).extension<MnTokens>()! and never names a colour. MaterialApp rebuilds on the provider change, and because lerp is implemented the switch animates rather than snapping.

The one discipline that makes or breaks it

No widget may hardcode a colour, radius or shadow. Not one. The moment a card says Color(0xFFC2410C) instead of reading the token, that card is stuck in Hearth forever and every other skin has a bug you'll find months later on someone else's phone. Worth a lint or a CI grep for hex literals outside the skin files.

Where a skin toggle genuinely earns its place here

  • Two parents, two tastes. The likeliest second installer is your wife, on a different phone, with different preferences. This costs you nothing and removes an argument.
  • Dawn / Dusk can be automatic. Not a preference — a function of the clock. The app is used at 06:15 and 20:00; letting it look like the hour it's used in is the one skin idea that is also a feature. Warm light before school, lamp light at bedtime.
  • It de-risks the choice you're making now. You don't have to be right about Hearth versus Aurora today. Ship two, watch which one you actually keep it on after a month, delete the other.

Where it doesn't

  • Screenshots and support. Every bug report arrives in a look you may not be running. Log the active skin with any diagnostics.
  • Accessibility multiplies. Contrast has to hold in all of them. Aurora's glass over a gradient is the risky one — translucency plus soft ink is where WCAG contrast quietly fails.
  • Notebook's cursive display font is charming and a legibility risk at 06:30. I'd cap it at the kicker and never use it for an item or a lock-screen line.

My picks from this batch

AuroraThe most immediately appealing of anything in the four files, and it photographs well. Watch contrast on the glass, and it does sit close to the calm/wellness shelf — the thing you rejected StillTime to avoid.
NotebookThe most apt. A school diary with tape and a teacher's red pen is the actual artifact families use. Strongest emotional fit for the product; keep the handwriting decorative only.
Dawn / DuskMy favourite, because it isn't really a skin — it's the app knowing what time it is. Warm morning light at 06:15, lamp light at 20:00. Delight that costs one if statement.

If I shipped three: Dawn/Dusk as the automatic default, Hearth for anyone who wants it flat and warm, Notebook as the charming option. Instrument and Riso stay as documents rather than skins — Instrument is a different architecture and Riso will date.
