A and B both answer "what do I need to know?". This one answers a different, more physical question — "is it out yet?" — because that is the question a parent actually acts on at 20:00 with a small child in the room.

The premise

Nobody misses colour dress because they didn't know. They miss it because at 07:12 the outfit isn't out and there is no time. Reading a card and doing the thing are different acts, and every product in this space stops at the first one.

So Direction C adds a third state after proposed and confirmed: laid out. The home screen is a picture of tomorrow — garment, box, bag, form — with a tick per object. The 20:00 alarm doesn't say "review"; it says put it out now. And it keeps asking until the ticks are filled.

Two consequences fall out. The interface becomes objects rather than text, which makes it readable at arm's length and across a kitchen. And it becomes showable to the child, which turns a chore into a bedtime ritual — the one audience every other app in this category ignores.

Honest assessment

This is the most opinionated of the three and the most likely to be wrong. Adventurous means load-bearing bets, so here are the bets:

  • The bet that pays regardless: the laid-out state. Even inside Direction A's warm cards, adding "is it out?" attacks the real failure. If you take one thing from this file, take that.
  • The bet that might not: objects over text. It's beautiful and fast when the item is a shirt or a snack box. It degrades the moment a circular says "bring ₹200 for the book fair" and there is no shape for that. Needs a graceful text fallback, and the fallback will be most of the long tail.
  • The bet that's pure upside but pure scope: kid mode. Genuinely novel, genuinely delightful, entirely optional. v1.1 at the earliest.
  • The risk in the whole direction: it is a strong aesthetic. Riso-poster editorial will look dated in three years in a way A's warmth and B's restraint will not. For a family app you'll use for a decade of schooling, that matters more than it would for a product with a redesign budget.

If I had to choose

A's palette and voice · B's architecture · C's laid-out state and physical verbs. Statement-first home showing tomorrow's objects with ticks, visible armed alarms beneath, list triage with Put out / Later / Bin, warm and rounded rather than riso-loud. That hybrid is what I'd actually build — but the three poles had to exist first for it to be a choice rather than a guess.
